# How to use these prompts

Working instructions for an AI assistant (Claude or comparable) that can read your
files and produce documents. Work in order — except that **prompt 02 (deadlines)
runs the moment you have any denial letter in hand**, before anything else.

| # | Prompt | Produces |
|---|--------|----------|
| 01 | paper-inventory | Every EOB, letter, prior-auth, and call logged and dated |
| 02 | deadlines-and-plan-type | The deadline board + which appeal machinery governs |
| 03 | denial-anatomy | Each denial dissected: reason, criteria, rights — verbatim |
| 04 | claim-file-request | The written request for the insurer's complete file |
| 05 | appeal-packet | The internal appeal: letter + physician statement + exhibits |
| 06 | external-review | The IRO submission after a final internal denial |
| 07 | regulators | State DOI / DOL-EBSA complaint built from the same bundle |
| 08 | adversarial-review | Attack pass before anything is sent |
| 09 | case-pdf-and-site | The packet as PDF; optional gated site for long sagas |
| 10 | tracking-and-follow-up | Proof-of-delivery discipline and the outcome ledger |

## Ground rules to give the assistant once, at the start

> You are helping me appeal health-insurance adverse determinations. Non-negotiable:
> 1. DEADLINES FIRST. Before drafting anything, compute every appeal window from
>    the letters and show me the board. If a deadline is near, we file a timely
>    rights-preserving appeal now and supplement later.
> 2. Never state a fact without a document or logged call behind it. A letter
>    proves only what it says; quote it verbatim.
> 3. You do not practice medicine. Medical-necessity statements come from my
>    clinicians, in their words. Your job is to hand them the plan's actual
>    criteria and assemble the record around their letter.
> 4. Identify my plan type (ERISA self-funded / fully-insured / Medicare
>    Advantage / Medicaid managed care) from my documents before citing any
>    appeal right, and tell me your confidence and basis.
> 5. Every count and date in any output is computed from the catalogue with an
>    assertion that fails on mismatch.
> 6. Keep an append-only decisions file recording every ruling I make.
> 7. This is high-stakes: when something needs a lawyer, a broker, or my state's
>    consumer-assistance program, say so plainly.

## What only you can decide

Whether to appeal at all, what medical information you are willing to put in
writing, whether family/employer get involved, when to escalate to a regulator,
and anything a professional should review. For urgent ongoing care, use the
expedited paths and get help — this method is strongest for the paper war, not
the emergency.
